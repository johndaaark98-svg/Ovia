Google Ads AI Max for Firms & SMEs: Control It or Lose

Google Ads AI Max is quietly redrawing who sees your ads, what they say and where the clicks land. For Italian professional firms and SMEs, handing full control to the algorithm is a risky move — and it often happens in silence.

Ovia editorial team·18 November 2026·11 min read·AI search and marketing

From September 2026 Google began automatically migrating many Search campaigns to AI Max — the new system that delegates keyword matching, ad copy generation and even landing-page selection to the algorithm. Dynamic Search Ads (DSA) will follow in February 2027. This is not an optional update: if you have not taken any action, some of these automations may already be live in your account.

For a firm of accountants, solicitors or employment consultants, and for an Italian SME, this change has three immediate practical consequences: advertising budget can be spent on irrelevant searches or searches for competitors; ad copy may contain claims that breach professional ethical codes; and your website may receive traffic on the wrong pages. Meanwhile, on AI search engines — from ChatGPT to Perplexity to Google AI Mode — the visibility game follows different rules entirely, where simply being mentioned is not enough: what counts is being recommended.

What Google Ads AI Max has already changed in your account

Google Ads AI Max is not a new product you choose to activate: it is an ongoing transition that has already affected, since September 2026, Search campaigns using automatically created assets and campaign-level broad match settings. Dynamic Search Ads will be brought into scope in February 2027. Search Engine Journal has documented this in detail, noting that the migration does not enable the same features in every campaign: two campaigns both showing 'AI Max active' can have very different levels of automation.

The three main levers of AI Max are search term matching, text customisation and Final URL expansion. The first can operate at ad-group level; the other two at campaign level. Before touching any setting, it is essential to understand which of these levers are already switched on in your account — otherwise it is impossible to assess what is actually driving performance.

For a professional firm, the most immediate impact involves ad copy: if automatic text customisation is enabled, Google can generate headlines and descriptions by drawing on existing keywords, existing ads and website content. The result can be an ad that is grammatically correct but inaccurate about services, pricing or areas of expertise — and the firm's principal remains responsible for everything that appears in the firm's name.

The invisible risk: where your money goes with AI Max

Automatic search term matching is where budget can evaporate fastest. AI Max uses broad match and keywordless matching technologies to intercept searches the algorithm deems relevant, even when they were never part of your original strategy. Research by Mike Ryan of Smarter Ecommerce across 383 million impressions showed that more than 25% of impressions attributed to exact-match keywords actually came from AI Max matching — not from the keyword you had selected.

In a single account analysed in the same study, competitor searches accounted for 69% of total Search impressions. For a professional firm working with a limited budget, this means almost all spend could be going on intercepting people who are actively searching for a competitor — a cost with no direct return.

Final URL expansion adds another layer of risk: when enabled, Google autonomously selects which page of your site to serve as the destination, even if that page has no contact form, is not designed for conversion, or relates to a service you are not promoting in that campaign. For a firm whose website also contains blog posts, news items or inactive service pages, the risk of wasting budget on unqualified traffic is very real. Campaign-level URL exclusions and ad-group-level URL inclusions are the tools to limit this — but they must be configured actively.

The positive note: Google reports that advertisers using the full AI Max feature set achieve on average 7% more conversions at a similar CPA compared with search term matching alone. However, that figure comes from Google itself and reflects the average. Independent research by Smarter Ecommerce across more than 250 campaigns found a median CPA 16% higher than the baseline, with results ranging from +42% to −35% ROAS. For an Italian firm or SME, relying on Google's average without measuring your own account is a gamble, not a strategy.

AI-generated ad copy: who is responsible?

Automatic text customisation allows Google to generate new headlines and descriptions by drawing on existing ads, keywords and landing-page content. For anyone running lead-generation campaigns for a professional firm, this creates a problem that goes beyond copy quality: an automatically generated ad could contain claims about fees, guaranteed outcomes or areas of expertise that do not reflect reality — or that breach the ethical code of the relevant professional body.

Google does allow advertisers to review and remove automatically generated assets. It has also introduced tools such as AI Brief and text disclaimers to give advertisers greater control over generated messages. But these tools only work if they are actively used: the algorithm's default settings know nothing about the Italian Bar Association's Code of Professional Conduct or the advertising rules applicable to chartered accountants.

There is a further point of attention for those using asset pinning in responsive search ads — the feature that locks a specific headline or description into a fixed position. According to Google's own documentation, pinning is not respected when Final URL expansion is active or when URL inclusions are in place: in those cases, Google may generate copy suited to the new landing page, overriding the assets you pinned. For campaigns that require mandatory disclaimers, partner-approved messaging or copy subject to strict regulatory constraints, Final URL expansion must be disabled. This is not an optional recommendation: it is risk management.

Important: If text customisation is active, Google may publish ads in your firm's name containing copy you have never approved. Check the generated assets in the dedicated section of Google Ads and remove any that are non-compliant. If your practice has ethical or regulatory obligations regarding advertising messages (law firms, accountants, employment consultants), consider disabling text customisation or setting up a precise AI Brief that explicitly excludes non-permitted claims.

How to find out whether AI search engines mention your firm

Google Ads AI Max concerns paid advertising. But visibility on AI search engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Gemini — is a separate arena, increasingly relevant for anyone searching for a professional service provider. Understanding how this second channel works is essential to avoid investing resources in the wrong place.

An analysis by SparkToro and Verso across 41,961 ChatGPT conversations from 12,321 US users (January–October 2026) found that only 6.9% of conversations contained purchase intent. Of all ChatGPT responses in the weighted sample, 87.9% cited no brand, product or company at all. SparkToro's Rand Fishkin concluded that the AI channel truly relevant to commercial visibility is Google AI Overviews and AI Mode — not ChatGPT itself, at least for now.

This does not mean ignoring ChatGPT or Perplexity: it means setting priorities correctly. Patrick Reinhart of Conductor, a guest on the Search Engine Journal podcast, describes the golden metric in AI search not as the number of times your firm is mentioned, but as the quality of the recommendation: does ChatGPT cite you as the first choice, mention you in passing alongside others, or — worse — describe you inaccurately or negatively? Each situation calls for a very different response.

Reinhart also flags a common practical problem: AI search engines may draw on outdated information held on third-party sites — forum threads, directories last updated years ago, articles containing incorrect data about services or pricing. If your firm is described inaccurately by a source that AI engines keep citing, the fix does not lie in updating your own website: it requires identifying that source and creating more authoritative content to displace it. What to measure to know whether AI visibility actually brings clients

The most common trap in AI search marketing is measuring everything that can be measured rather than measuring what matters. A useful framework, described in TransPerfect Digital's analysis on Search Engine Journal, distinguishes four levels of measurement that are worth keeping separate for a professional firm or SME.

The first level is monitored visibility: the mentions and citations you can track using defined prompts. The second is recorded referral traffic: the visits your analytics system attributes to AI search engines when the referral is available. The third consists of directional signals: shifts in branded searches or enquiries that suggest a possible AI-driven effect, without the ability to prove causation. The fourth is estimated impact: projections that depend on assumptions rather than direct observation.

For Google Ads AI Max, measurement must go beyond conversion volume. If you are collecting leads, the key question is: are those leads qualifying? Are they becoming clients? If you are only measuring cost per completed form, you may be paying for contacts that never result in a mandate. For e-commerce businesses or SMEs selling products, revenue and margin per product are more relevant than conversion volume. Reinhart puts it well: business leaders do not ask 'where do I appear?'; they ask 'how am I described, and are those descriptions accurate?'

Before changing budgets, campaigns or content, it is worth answering four questions: are we measuring the searches our clients actually make? Are we interpreting data in the context of our own positioning? Which findings warrant concrete action? What impact can we demonstrate with observed data — not estimates? This applies equally to managing Google Ads AI Max and to building an AI search visibility strategy.

Is your firm's website ready to be found and cited by AI?

Google Ads AI Max and AI search engine visibility share a common root: both depend on the quality of the content on your website. Final URL expansion uses your site's pages to select ad destinations. AI search engines use those same pages — alongside third-party sources — to build their responses. A poorly structured site with generic pages, no structured data and no clear distinction between services on offer is a liability on both fronts.

For AI visibility, the problem is not purely technical. The TransPerfect Digital analysis identifies a frequent trap: many firms measure their AI visibility using branded prompts ('XYZ firm in London'), obtain reassuring scores, but never measure the questions clients ask when they do not yet know the firm — comparisons between professionals, searches by specialisation, questions about costs and procedures. These are the queries that bring in new clients, and a firm often does not appear in those answers at all.

For Google Ads AI Max, the practical advice is to configure URL exclusions to protect the pages on your site you do not want used as paid campaign destinations: blog articles, inactive service pages, institutional pages with no call-to-action. For AI visibility, the work is different but complementary: structure service pages with direct answers to the most frequently asked questions, update contact information across all relevant directories, and build consistent, up-to-date third-party citations. Strategy comes before default settings

✦ The Ovia view: strategy and security before the tool

At Ovia we work with Italian professional firms and SMEs who find themselves in exactly this position: they have activated Google Ads — or some automation platform — without a measurement strategy, and realise too late that budget has been spent on irrelevant traffic or that AI search engines are finding the site in the wrong way. The problem is almost never the tool itself: it is the absence of a conscious decision about what to automate, what to exclude and how to measure.

That is why our starting point is never 'which AI Max feature should we enable', but rather 'what is the campaign objective, who needs to find this firm, and what should happen after the click'. The same applies to AI visibility: we first define the prompts that reflect the real questions the firm's clients ask, then we measure the gap, then we act — on the website, the content and the third-party sources.

Security is the other half of the work. Who has access to the firm's Google Ads account? Who approves automatically generated copy before it goes live? If conversion data flows through Google Tag Manager, who ensures that client information is not being collected in a way that is non-compliant with the UK GDPR or Italian data protection rules? These are not technical questions: they are governance questions, and they fall to the firm's principal — not the algorithm.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Ads AI Max worthwhile for a professional firm or SME?

It depends on configuration and measurement. Google reports an average of 7% more conversions for advertisers using the full feature set, but independent research by Smarter Ecommerce across more than 250 campaigns found a median CPA 16% higher than the baseline, with very wide variation between accounts. For firms with limited budgets and strict ethical obligations regarding advertising messages, the prudent approach is to enable AI Max gradually, using controlled experiments, and to measure lead quality — not just volume.

How can I control what Google Ads AI Max decides on my behalf?

Go into each Search campaign in Google Ads and check the status of three features: search term matching, text customisation and Final URL expansion. Then download the search terms report filtered by AI Max source and review which queries you are intercepting. Finally, in the optimised assets section, read every automatically generated piece of copy and remove anything that does not comply with your positioning or regulatory obligations.

Which Google Ads AI Max settings should I audit right now?

The three priorities are: Final URL expansion (disable it if your site has pages unsuitable for conversion, or if you use mandatory disclaimers in your copy); text customisation (review generated assets and use AI Brief to guide messaging); and negative keyword exclusions (add competitors, informational terms far from conversion, and brand keywords to non-brand campaigns). Also check whether your asset pinning is being respected: it will not be if Final URL expansion or URL inclusions are active.

How do I find out whether my firm is being cited by AI search engines such as ChatGPT or Perplexity?

The basic method is to manually test a set of prompts that reflect the real questions your potential clients ask — not just the firm's name, but also queries by specialisation, geographic area and type of problem. Test on ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity separately, as each model behaves differently. Note whether you are cited, in what position, with what description, and whether you are recommended as the first choice or merely mentioned in passing.

What should I measure to know whether AI visibility is actually bringing in clients?

Keep four levels separate: monitored visibility (mentions in tracked prompts), recorded referral traffic (visits attributed to AI search engines in analytics), directional signals (shifts in branded searches) and estimated impact (assumption-based projections). Only the first two are observed data. To assess real commercial impact, compare leads or enquiries from AI channels against those from other channels in terms of qualification rate and conversion to clients.

Is my firm's website optimised to be found and cited by AI?

A site suited to AI search engines has service pages with direct answers to the most common client questions, up-to-date structured data, consistent contact information across all relevant directories, and content that clearly distinguishes specialisations and geographic areas. Updating the site alone is not enough: you also need to verify that third-party sources (directories, forums, sector websites) describe the firm accurately, because AI search engines use them as sources alongside the official website.

How do I prevent AI from generating incorrect or non-compliant ad copy in my firm's name?

There are three levers: first, disable text customisation if regulatory or ethical constraints are strict; second, set up a precise AI Brief that explicitly excludes non-permitted categories of claim (guaranteed outcomes, competitor comparisons, unverified fees); third, establish a periodic review of generated assets — at least every two weeks — with responsibility assigned to a named individual within the firm. The algorithm's defaults do not know the rules of your profession: whoever manages the account must.

Conclusion

Google Ads AI Max and AI search engines are reshaping how an Italian professional firm or SME is found, evaluated and chosen by new clients. Automation is not wrong in itself: the problem is relying on default settings without a strategy, without measuring the quality of results, and without verifying that automatically generated messages comply with regulatory and professional ethical obligations.

The good news is that the control levers exist — URL exclusions, negative keywords, asset reviews, AI Brief — and that measuring AI visibility, though imperfect, is achievable without enterprise-level tools. What makes the difference is deciding consciously what to control actively and what to leave to the algorithm, rather than discovering the answer after the budget has run out.
